What the locked numbers mean

Plain-English read of the LOCKED MODEL OUTPUT. These are full-year model values and do not change with the visual controls below. Sources tagged by evidence tier.
LWBS, walkouts: 20.40%
Share of ED arrivals who leave without being seen. It rises when the ward stays full and admitted patients board in the ED, so the front door backs up behind the back door. Model output.
Ward occupancy: 88.02%
Average share of staffed ward beds in use. The higher it runs, the less slack there is to admit the next patient. Model output.
ALC bed-day share: 11.62%
Share of ward bed-days held by Alternate Level of Care patients, who are medically ready to leave but waiting for a place to go. The model's pALC is calibrated to the provincial 21.3% ALC day-share, not measured at CBRH. Calibrated, Tier B. No Cape Breton ALC percentage is presented as measured.
LTC placement wait: 111 days
Median time from ALC designation to a long-term care bed. This dwell is what fills the ward, and halving it is what drains it. Cape Breton County median, Tier A (DHW).
Home-care wait: 8 days
Median wait to start home care for those routed to the community rather than to long-term care. National median, Tier B (CIHI).
Avoided cost is reported as a system or cohort figure and is always paired with the harm it represents (Obermeyer pairing). No per-patient dollar values.

OPOR + secured API, forward extension

One Person One Record connected to ED triage through a secured API means the patient's record travels and is secured across the ecosystem: ED, community clinic, long-term care, home care. It secures the system through a single source of truth, controlled access with an audit trail, and fewer handoff failures where data is lost or re-keyed today. It strengthens both doors: the front door hands a diverted patient's triage note to the receiving clinic; the back door moves the record and care plan with an ALC patient at placement. It is not in the model and no number here depends on it.
