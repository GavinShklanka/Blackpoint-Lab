The decision room, on the hardest live case: the submarine

This is not a boat being built at Irving. TKMS is preferred supplier only, not signed, and Canada has not sited assembly CA FACT. What this room depicts is the negotiation window, which runs to the end of 2027: design and requirement decisions on this class are being argued across Canada, Germany, and Norway right now. That makes it the most multi-party, high-consequence, physical decision problem in Canadian defence today, and that is why it is the demonstration case. What I want to do, and why it is feasible

What I want to do.

Build one room where the people who decide can stand around the same 3D vessel, see the current design, and see who approved it, when, and against which requirement. Then run one real decision through it and measure what happened.

Why it matters.

The first River-class ship is due in the early 2030s. The expensive delays on builds like this come from design changes landing late and work done out of order, not from a shortage of welders. Those are decision problems. Nobody is measuring how long a decision actually takes on this programme, so nobody can say what it costs.

Why it is an opportunity.

Before a single headset goes on, reconstructing how long past decisions took, and whether their approval records were complete, is new information. If the room works, we have proof. If it does not, we still have the first measured picture of decision latency on the programme. Both outcomes are worth the money. That is why it is worth starting.

What I can do now.

Build the room in Unreal Engine on our LED volume and headsets. Model the vessel, the role views, and the decision record. Write the measurement plan and register the thresholds before we run.

What I need from a partner.

Five to eight already-closed decisions to reconstruct the baseline. One live decision. Ninety minutes with the people who normally sign it off.

What I will not claim.

That this is proven. One decision is one decision. The threshold is written down first, and I stop if it fails.

What we do not have. Read this before approving.

  • No deployment of Perspective-Aware AI anywhere in shipbuilding. The decision room is a hypothesis with a test plan.
  • No resolution between DND’s $56-60B budget and PBO’s $84.5B estimate (Figure 2).
  • One measured XR result total, at N=22, in civilian welding. That is the entire proven base.
The close. Fund the two measured pilots, hold the written thresholds, and stop anything that fails them. A repeatable, auditable way to decide is the prize; the headset is incidental.
